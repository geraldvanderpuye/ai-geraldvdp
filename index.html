AI-native doesn’t mean putting AI into the business. It means reconsidering what the business could be.

01 — A different product

The lesson became a conversation.

Duolingo already had a good product: short language lessons that could travel anywhere. AI gave it a different direction.

Instead of only completing exercises, a learner can now hold a live conversation with a character who responds in real time, remembers what was discussed and gives feedback afterwards.

Before
A lesson available anywhere.
After
A speaking partner available whenever you need one.

02 — A different company

The helpdesk became the service.

Intercom built software for the people answering customer questions. Then it built Fin — an AI agent designed to answer and resolve those questions itself.

In 2026, the change became literal. Intercom renamed the company after Fin. The original helpdesk remains a product. The AI agent is the direction.

Before
Software that helped a support team.
After
A service measured by the questions it resolves.

03 — A different founder

The software customer became the builder.

I reached a point where I was still paying for software I could now build myself.

So I built Loop — an AI follow-up tool for membership businesses. I built it solo. It made £5k in its first 10 days and now replaces large parts of my own software stack.

Rebuilding my own websites saved me roughly £15k and brought about five times the traffic. The important change wasn’t speed. It was becoming capable of making exactly what the business needed.

I don’t just teach this. I build with it.
